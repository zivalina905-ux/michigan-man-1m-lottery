How a Michigan man claims a $1 million lottery prize

A $1 million top prize is far above the retail-claim limit, so it always ends at Lottery headquarters in Lansing. Here is the exact process, step by step.

  1. Sign the back of the ticket immediately.

    A lottery ticket is a bearer instrument — whoever holds it can claim it. Sign it, photograph both sides, and store it somewhere flat and safe.

  2. Verify it yourself before telling anyone.

    Scan the ticket with the Michigan Lottery app or ask a retailer to check it. Confirm the game, prize level, and that the ticket isn't expired (instant games expire one year after the game's official end date).

  3. Call the Lottery before traveling.

    For prizes of $100,000 and up — every $1 million win — call the Michigan Lottery at 844-887-6836 to arrange an appointment at headquarters: 101 N. Walnut Street, Lansing, MI 48933.

  4. Bring your documents.

    Government-issued photo ID, your Social Security number, and the completed claim form. If you're claiming through a trust or legal entity, bring the paperwork and expect extra review.

  5. Understand the withholding.

    The Lottery automatically withholds 24% federal and 4.25% Michigan state tax on prizes over $5,000. On a $1 million lump sum that's about $282,500 withheld — you receive roughly $717,500 at claim time.

  6. Get paid — lump sum or annuity.

    Most instant-game $1 million top prizes are paid as a one-time lump sum; some games and draw jackpots pay annuities spread over years. Powerball and Mega Millions jackpot winners get a 60-day window to choose cash value vs. annuity. Payment typically arrives within a few weeks after verification.

⚠️
Deadline math: instant-game prizes expire 1 year after the game ends; draw-game prizes expire 180 days after the drawing. Unclaimed $1M prizes roll back into the School Aid Fund — don't let your ticket become public-school money.
