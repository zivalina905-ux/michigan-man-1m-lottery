The Kenly winner: one $30 ticket after work

Sourced from the NC Education Lottery's official announcements and national coverage.

Harmon's win is the cleanest possible version of the scratch-ticket dream. No system, no lucky ritual, no second-chance promotion: an iron worker stopping at a truck stop on the ride home from a shift, buying his usual Pick 3 and Pick 4 numbers, then adding one $30 Supreme Riches ticket on a whim. The NC Education Lottery's own release put his quote on record: "I went home and fired up my grill and I said, 'Well I might as well scratch my ticket.' I looked down and I said, 'Woah!'"

He claimed the prize at the lottery's Raleigh headquarters — standard procedure for jackpot-tier instant wins — and became the third of the game's four $10 million top-prize winners, but the first whose win made the state's all-time scratch-off record an easy headline: $10 million was, and remains, the biggest instant-ticket prize the NC Education Lottery had paid out.

His reaction to the money was unusually level-headed for a record jackpot: "I'm thankful, I really am. I believe the Lord saved that for me… He's got plans for me." And his plan was almost aggressively normal — keep working, retire in a few years, take the kids and family on a cruise once the pandemic calmed down, and, in his own words, "I'll still be the same old me."

$300,000,000 Supreme Riches: the game that paid three $10M winners

The game behind the record: a $30 premium scratch-off launched April 2019, printing four $10 million top prizes with a $500,000-a-year-for-20-years annuity as the alternative. It was NC's biggest instant-game ever at launch, and the top-prize roll call reads like a case study:

  • May 2019 — James Belich. The first $10M claim, won on a lunch break; said he wanted to give his kids "a brighter future."
  • September 2019 — Joseph Keefer. A Johnston County home painter who credited his brother's luck and split the prize with him.
  • May 2020 — Wade Harmon. The third top prize — the record-setting claim covered here.

Every one of the three chose the lump sum over the annuity. That's not a coincidence of personalities; it's what the math section below is about. And it rhymes with what we see across states: the Georgia record jackpot crowd, the $1.4M Sheetz winner, and the Midland $5M scratch champion all faced the same immediate-cash-versus-streams decision — instant-game top prizes are almost always structured so the lump sum is the headline number.

One more thing worth knowing about scratch-off records in general: they're printed, not rolled like draw-game jackpots. A $10M scratch ceiling exists because the game's prize table caps it there — which is why a state record on a scratch ticket moves in $10M-class steps rather than the nine-figure escalation of Powerball jackpots.

Why did the number drop so far? Two reasons. Federal: prizes over $5,000 carry mandatory 24% withholding, and because lottery income stacks on top of other income, winners in the top bracket can owe up to 37% in total at filing time. State: North Carolina taxes lottery winnings as ordinary income at its flat rate — 5.25% in 2020 (the rate has been cut in stages since, to 3.99% by 2026 under scheduled reductions). Compare that with the zero-state-tax Texas winner, or Pennsylvania's 3.07% flat tax — geography is worth real millions on a record prize.

NC claim rules: the 90-day scratch-off clock most players miss

  • Scratch-offs: 90 days after the game ends NC draw games give you 180 days from the drawing — but scratch-off tickets only get 90 days from the announced end of the game. Shorter than Texas (180), Florida (60) is tighter still. When a game's end is announced, the lottery publishes each game's last claim date.
  • Prize-tier venues Up to $599: any NC lottery retailer. Larger prizes: regional offices or headquarters in Raleigh — that's where Harmon claimed $10 million. Bring the signed original ticket, a claim form and photo ID; jackpot-tier claims are by appointment.
  • Mailing a claim? The ticket must ARRIVE in time In NC, a postmark does not save you — the ticket has to be received by the deadline. A claim mailed on day 89 is void if it lands on day 91. For seven-figure tickets, claim in person.
  • What happens if nobody claims it The prize is void and the money goes to the state — education funds, in NC's case. Scale check: about $40 million in NC lottery winnings went unclaimed in a single recent year, and the lottery has posted unclaimed $1 million tickets more than once. Somebody printed those winners; nobody cashed them.
  • No anonymity in NC Unlike the confidentiality election Texas gives $1M+ winners, North Carolina is a public-record state — name, hometown and game get released. That's the only reason this story has a human face at all.

Smart moves right after a record scratch-off win

  • Sign it, photo it, calm down. The ticket is a bearer document. Harmon went home, grilled, then scratched — the delay cost him nothing; losing an unsigned ticket in a parking lot would have.
  • Check which game you're holding. If the game has ended (or is ending), the 90-day NC clock is already running. The lottery publishes last-claim dates per game — look it up before you assume you have "six months."
  • Model the lump sum honestly. $10M prize → $6M cash → ~$4.2M after tax is the real chain. Plan around the number that clears, not the billboard.
  • Claim big prizes in person. Raleigh HQ for jackpot-tier; never mail a ticket you can't afford to lose — and in NC the mail clock doesn't care about your postmark anyway.
  • Disputes and misreads happen. See how 252 contested tickets were resolved in our Hoosier Lottery technical issue coverage — verification paperwork matters at record tier.
  • Repeat wins are real but rare. Our two-prizes-one-station story covers the odds of lightning striking the same buyer twice — don't budget for it.

Did a North Carolina man really win a record lottery jackpot with a scratch ticket?
Yes. Wade Harmon, an iron worker from Kenly in Johnston County, won $10 million on the NC Education Lottery's $300,000,000 Supreme Riches scratch-off in May 2020 — the largest scratch-off prize ever paid by the North Carolina Education Lottery at that time. What did the winner take home after taxes?
Harmon declined the $500,000-a-year-for-20-years annuity and took the $6 million lump sum. After federal withholding and North Carolina income tax, he took home just over $4.2 million. North Carolina taxes lottery winnings at its flat state income-tax rate — 5.25% at the time, since reduced — on top of the 24% federal withholding that applies to prizes over $5,000.
What was the $300,000,000 Supreme Riches game?

Wade Harmon claimed the third of the four $10 million prizes. James Belich claimed the first in May 2019 and home painter Joseph Keefer the second in September 2019; all three winners chose the lump sum.
How long do you have to claim an NC scratch-off prize?
North Carolina scratch-off tickets must be claimed within 90 days after the game's end is announced — shorter than the 180-day window for NC draw games. Mailed claims must physically arrive by the deadline; a postmark does not count. After the deadline the ticket is void, and roughly $40 million in NC lottery winnings went unclaimed in a single recent year.
Can North Carolina lottery winners stay anonymous?
No. North Carolina is a public-record state: the NC Education Lottery releases winners' names, hometowns and games, which is why we know Wade Harmon's name and story at all. Big winners' realistic privacy options are limited to claiming quickly, controlling their own messaging, and getting advice before going public.
Where do you claim a $10 million NC Lottery prize?
At NC Education Lottery headquarters in Raleigh, which is where Harmon claimed his record prize. Prizes up to $599 can be cashed at any retailer; larger prizes move to regional offices or Raleigh, and jackpot-tier claims require the signed original ticket, a claim form and photo ID.
Lump sum or annuity: what did the record winners choose?
All three of the first Supreme Riches $10 million winners took the lump sum. Harmon's math: $6 million upfront (about $4.2 million after taxes) versus $500,000 a year for 20 years ($10 million total before tax). The same tradeoff we break down in our annuity-vs-lump-sum guide — liquidity and investment control now, versus guaranteed payments later.
