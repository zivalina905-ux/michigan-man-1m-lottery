The tax math: what a $5 million Texas winner actually keeps

This is the part where Texas winners quietly smile. Texas has no state income tax, so the state takes nothing off the top of a lottery prize. The only automatic deduction is federal: 24% withholding on any prize over $5,000.

  • Gross prize: $5,000,000
  • Federal withholding (24%): −$1,200,000
  • Check at claim time: ≈ $3,800,000

The catch is the same one every big winner learns in April: withholding is a down payment on the tax bill, not the bill itself. Lottery winnings stack on top of your other income for the year, and the top federal bracket is 37% — so a winner with other income could owe up to roughly $1.85 million in total federal tax on the $5 million, meaning the $3.8M check might need another check to the IRS. A good CPA before claiming is not optional math; it's the difference between a plan and a surprise.

⚖️Texas's zero-state-tax treatment is the mirror image of states we've covered: Pennsylvania takes 3.07% flat, Ohio withholds 4% plus 2.75% at filing, and Maryland — our Pick 3/4/5 guide state — tiers its tax up to 8%+ on big prizes. The same $5 million ticket is worth very different money depending on where you scratch it.

Can Texas lottery winners stay anonymous? Yes — above $1 million

Most states treat a lottery win like a public-records event: name, city, game, released to anyone who asks. Texas is the exception that made anonymity famous. Under the Texas Lottery's administrative rules (16 TAC §401.324), winners of $1 million or more may elect confidentiality before or at claim time, and the lottery then may not disclose identifying information.

The fine print: the election is for $1M+ prizes only — a $500,000 winner has no such option — and it doesn't shield you from everything (court orders, certain disclosures, your own big mouth). But in practice it works: walk into Austin HQ as "a Texas resident," walk out $3.8 million richer and legally invisible. That's exactly the path the Midland winner took, and it's why this article can tell you the what and where but not the who.

How to claim a $5 million prize in Texas

  • Step 1 — Sign and secure the ticket Sign the back immediately. A Texas Lottery ticket is a bearer instrument — whoever holds it can claim it. Photograph both sides, then store the original somewhere boring and safe while you decide what's next.
  • Step 2 — Verify at a retailer or the app Any Texas Lottery retailer can scan it; the Texas Lottery app's ticket checker does the same without a counter. For a five- or six-figure winner, expect the retailer to pay small prizes and point you to a claim center for the rest.
  • Step 3 — Match the prize tier to the venue Up to $599: any retailer or claim center. Up to roughly $2.5 million: a Texas Lottery claim center. A $5 million top prize: headquarters in Austin — claim form, original ticket, photo ID. Appointments are expected for jackpot-tier claims.
  • Step 4 — Mind the 180 days Draw games: 180 days from the drawing. Scratch-offs: 180 days from the announced end of the game. Miss it and the prize is forfeited to the state — Texas has paid out billions in prizes since 1992, and the unclaimed pile has topped $1 billion. If you're holding a ticket from a game that just announced its end, that's your clock ticking.
  • Step 5 — Elect anonymity (if $1M+) Submit the confidentiality election before the claim is processed. This is a before-the-fact choice; once your name is released, you can't un-ring that bell.
  • Step 6 — Assemble the team, then the claim CPA, attorney, financial advisor — in that order, before you sign anything else. Then claim. Large prizes pay after verification; the Texas Lottery's standard guidance is weeks, not hours, for seven-figure claims.
