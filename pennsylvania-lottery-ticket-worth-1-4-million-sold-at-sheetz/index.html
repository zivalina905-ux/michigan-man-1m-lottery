The win: a $2 ticket, $1.42 million jackpot

Someone bought a $2 Match 6 Lotto ticket on a Wednesday at the Sheetz where Route 30 meets Carpenter Lane in North Huntingdon — and walked out with what turned out to be a $1.42 million jackpot winner. The Pennsylvania Lottery announced the sale, noted the identity stays unknown until the prize is claimed and validated, and set the clock: one year from the Jan. 14 drawing to claim.

North Huntingdon sits in Westmoreland County, just southeast of Pittsburgh — Sheetz country. The chain is one of Pennsylvania's highest-volume lottery retailers, which is why its stores keep appearing in jackpot headlines across the state.

The $10,000 Sheetz bonus

Pennsylvania pays its retailers for selling winners — $10,000 for a Match 6 jackpot ticket. Sheetz collected that bonus automatically when the Lottery validated the sale. It's the same bonus structure behind other PA retail stories: GetGo, Wawa, and gas-station counters across the state have all collected five-figure checks for selling million-dollar tickets.

For the retailer, the bonus plus the foot traffic ("the store that sold the $1.4M ticket") is real money and real marketing. For players, it's a reminder: any counter can sell the next one — there's no lucky store, just volume and math.

A $1.42 million claim is a headquarters appointment: the winner (or their attorney/trust, if structured) brings the signed original ticket and claim form to Middletown, where the Lottery validates, verifies identity and Social Security number, and processes payment — typically within a few weeks of a complete claim.

Steps for this specific winner (or any PA jackpot holder):

  1. Sign the back of the ticket now.

    An unsigned ticket is a bearer instrument — possession is nine-tenths of the claim.

  2. Photograph both sides and store it flat.

    Copies are backup evidence for mail claims and disputes; the original must remain unaltered.

  3. Call PA Lottery headquarters before traveling.

    Prizes over $25,000 require an appointment at Middletown — walk-ins aren't processed for jackpot-level claims.

  4. Assemble the paperwork.

    Claim form, government photo ID, Social Security number — matching names, no exceptions.

  5. Talk to a CPA before choosing lump sum vs. any structured option.

    PA prizes are taxed; see below. Withholding at claim is only the down payment on the real bill.

The one-year clock: why unclaimed jackpots happen

Every state's biggest lottery horror story is the same: a jackpot ticket quietly expires. Pennsylvania's one-year window gives this winner until mid-January 2027 — but the pattern of lost jackpots is well documented: tickets left in jackets, forgotten in gloveboxes, thrown out by family members cleaning up.

  • Check every ticket against the draw date — not just the latest results page.
  • Old tickets can still win. A ticket from months ago checked today against the wrong date is how people throw away millions.
  • If the ticket is damaged or lost, contact the Lottery immediately — validation without the physical ticket is possible in rare documented cases, but never guaranteed. Our Hoosier Lottery technical issue coverage shows what ticket disputes look like at scale.

Unclaimed PA prizes don't vanish into nothing — they're redirected by state law to older-adult programs, the Lottery's legislated beneficiary. But for the ticket holder, "the state got it" is cold comfort: claim early, claim once, claim right.

Pennsylvania $1.4M Sheetz ticket — FAQ

Where was the $1.4 million Pennsylvania Lottery ticket sold?

The Pennsylvania Lottery confirmed the store sold a $1.42 million jackpot-winning Match 6 Lotto ticket from the January 14, 2026 drawing, and awarded Sheetz a $10,000 bonus for selling it.
What were the winning Match 6 numbers?

What are the odds of winning the Pennsylvania Match 6 jackpot?
1 in 4,661,272 — among the best jackpot odds of any U.S. lotto-style draw game, roughly 63 times better than Powerball. Overall odds of winning any prize are about 1 in 5.9. The $2 ticket price includes two lines and automatic extras per PA's Match 6 format.
How long does the winner have to claim the $1.42 million?
One year from the January 14, 2026 drawing. Prizes over $25,000 must be claimed at Pennsylvania Lottery headquarters in Middletown by appointment; mail claims are accepted with the signed ticket and claim form.
How much tax will the winner pay?
Pennsylvania withholds its 3.07% flat personal income tax on lottery prizes, and the IRS withholds 24% federal on prizes over $5,000. On $1.42 million, that's roughly $43,600 state plus $340,800 federal withheld at claim — with the final bill set at filing time.
Why did Sheetz get a $10,000 bonus?
The Pennsylvania Lottery pays retailers a bonus for selling jackpot-winning tickets — $10,000 for a Match 6 jackpot like this one. Sheetz is one of PA's biggest lottery retailers; its stores have sold multiple million-dollar tickets across the state.
