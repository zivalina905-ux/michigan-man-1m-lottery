The drive-thru myth: why "lucky stores" aren't lucky

Players chase stores that sold winners — Ray's included. The honest mechanics of every drive-thru lottery prize story:

  • Every ticket's odds are identical. A Black Titanium scratch-off has the same 1-in-3.54 any-prize odds whether bought at Ray's, Meijer, or a gas station in Toledo. Fixed-prize lots are distributed statewide at printing, not per store — there is no lucky store, just volume and arithmetic.
  • Volume creates the illusion. Stores selling thousands of tickets per week produce winners by arithmetic. Ray's appearing in both the incentive program AND the winners column is the same correlation: foot traffic.
  • What actually improves your position: playing games with better odds (see our Lucky Day Lotto odds breakdown), checking remaining top prizes before buying, scanning every ticket, and signing it immediately.

Where store choice does matter practically: cashing limits. Ohio retailers pay up to $599 instantly; Super Retailers pay up to $5,000 same-day, and racinos pay up to $25,000. A winner planning to cash a $140K prize at a drive-thru window should know it's a claim-form appointment instead — the window's 2% commission only covers prizes small enough to cash on the spot.
