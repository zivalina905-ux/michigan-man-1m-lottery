Maryland Pick games Midday — quick answer

Quick answer The Maryland Lottery Midday drawing for Pick 3, Pick 4 and Pick 5 happens at 12:27 p.m. ET Monday–Friday (12:28 p.m. weekends), with sales closing at 12:24 p.m. Pick 3 pays $500 Straight on $1; Pick 4 pays $5,000; Pick 5 pays $50,000. Tickets cost $0.50 or $1 per bet, prizes are fixed, and winners have 182 days to claim. The Midday session is separate from the Evening draw (7:56 p.m., or 8:10 p.m. Sundays) — a Midday ticket only plays the Midday drawing.

All times Eastern. Note the weekend quirk: Midday shifts by one minute, and Sunday's Evening draw runs later than the rest of the week.

Three practical points about the md lottery midday numbers routine:

  • The cutoff is hard. Sales stop at 12:24 p.m. sharp; a ticket bought at 12:25 plays the next session, not the one you intended.
  • Mark your session. Midday and Evening are separate drawings with separate results. Always check your ticket against the session printed on it — the leading cause of "false wins" is comparing a Midday ticket to Evening numbers.
  • Results post online at mdlottery.com right after each drawing, and the lottery streams the draws live on its Watch the Drawings page.

How to play Maryland Pick 3, Pick 4 and Pick 5

  1. Pick your game and your digits.

    Pick 3: three digits (000–999). Pick 4: four digits. Pick 5: five digits. Each position is 0–9, and leading zeros count — 0-7-3 is a valid Pick 3 play, not "73".

  2. Choose a bet type.

    Straight, Box, Straight/Box (Pick 3 & 4 only), Combo, or partial-position bets — Front Pair/Back Pair on Pick 3, and Front 3/Back 3/Front 4/Back 4 on Pick 5.

  3. Set your stake: $0.50 or $1.

    Straight/Box is a fixed $1 (50¢ Straight + 50¢ Box). Combo buys every Straight arrangement as separate bets, so the price scales with your digits.

  4. Select Midday, Evening, or both.

    Multi-draw and advance play let you cover future sessions; count your panels × sessions × days before paying — three $1 plays on both sessions is $6 a day.

  5. Verify the printed ticket before leaving.

    Check game, digits, bet type, stake and session — the printed ticket, not your playslip or app draft, is the only valid proof of entry. Then sign the back.

Each added digit multiplies the exact-order possibilities tenfold — that's why the prizes scale $500 → $5,000 → $50,000 while the odds scale at exactly the same rate. The risk and reward move together; there's no "same game with a bigger guaranteed return."

Two Maryland-specific name traps worth knowing: Pick 5 is not Bonus Match 5 (a separate draw game where you pick five distinct numbers from a field — different matrix entirely), and a Pick 5 ticket's first three digits are not a Pick 3 entry. Each game is checked against its own drawing, its own session, its own bet type.

Straight/Box math warning: an exact-order 6-way Pick 3 Straight/Box pays $290 — not $580. You can't add a full $500 Straight to a full $80 Box, because each component stake is only 50¢. Same logic on 3-way: $330 exact ($250 + $80), $80 for any other covered order.

Maryland bet types, decoded — including MD Pick 3 Straight/Box

  • Straight — your digits in exact order. Highest payout, 1-in-1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 depending on game.
  • Box — your digits in any order. Fewer arrangements = bigger prize: 2-2-8 has three possible orders (3-way), 2-5-8 has six (6-way). Repeated digits narrow the coverage.
  • Straight/Box — one $1 ticket, two half-stakes: exact order wins both components, other covered orders win the Box half. Available on Pick 3 and Pick 4 only.
  • Combo — every Straight arrangement as a separate bet, so any order wins a full Straight prize. More coverage, proportionally higher price.
  • Pairs & partials — Front/Back Pair on Pick 3 ($50 on $1, 1-in-100 — the easiest win in the Maryland Pick family); Front 3/Back 3 and Front 4/Back 4 on Pick 5 mirror the Pick 3 and Pick 4 Straight prizes.

Maryland Lottery Pick 3 Pick 4 Pick 5 Midday — FAQ

What time is the Maryland Lottery Midday drawing?
The Maryland Lottery Midday drawing for Pick 3, Pick 4 and Pick 5 is held at 12:27 p.m. Eastern Time Monday through Friday, and 12:28 p.m. on Saturday and Sunday. Sales close at 12:24 p.m. every day, so get your ticket in about three minutes before the draw.
How much does Maryland Pick 5 pay?
A $1 Straight Pick 5 bet pays $50,000 for matching all five digits in exact order — odds of 1 in 100,000. A 50-cent Straight pays $25,000. Box prizes scale by the number of digit arrangements: a 5-way Box pays $10,000, a 120-way Box pays $400 on a $1 play.
What are the payouts for Maryland Pick 3 and Pick 4?
A $1 Pick 3 Straight pays $500 (1 in 1,000); Box pays $160 for a 3-way or $80 for a 6-way match. A $1 Pick 4 Straight pays $5,000 (1 in 10,000); Box pays $1,200 for a 4-way down to $200 for a 24-way match. Straight/Box on Pick 3 and Pick 4 costs a fixed $1 and pays both components on an exact-order hit.
Does Maryland Pick 5 offer Straight/Box?
No. Maryland's official bet menu offers Straight, Box, Front 3, Back 3, Front 4, Back 4 and Combo for Pick 5 — but not Straight/Box. Straight/Box is only available on Pick 3 and Pick 4 tickets.
How long do I have to claim a Maryland Pick 3, Pick 4 or Pick 5 prize?
182 days from the drawing date. Retailers can pay prizes up to $600 on the spot; larger claims require photo ID and a Social Security number, and prizes over $25,000 must be claimed by appointment at Maryland Lottery headquarters in Baltimore.
Is the Midday drawing different from the Evening drawing in Maryland?
Same games, different session. Evening drawings run at 7:56 p.m. Monday through Saturday and 8:10 p.m. on Sunday. A Midday ticket is only eligible for the Midday drawing — the sessions never share results, so always check your ticket against the session printed on it.
How much does Maryland tax Pick game winnings?
Maryland Lottery automatically withholds taxes on larger prizes — roughly 24% federal and 8.75–9.25% Maryland state depending on the prize size. Published payout amounts are gross figures before withholding; your final obligation depends on your total income when you file.
