What was the Hoosier Lottery technical issue?

Quick answer In June 2026, the Hoosier Lottery's newly launched $5 Space Invaders Cash Invasion scratch-off went on sale with a printing error in its play area: symbols on some tickets were misprinted so that losing tickets appeared to show winning matches. The lottery halted sales within days, told apparent winners their tickets didn't validate, and spent the summer reviewing protest forms. On August 28, 2026, it announced goodwill payments to 252 players holding misprinted tickets — up to $10,000 each, above what Indiana law requires.

The Hoosier Lottery's own advisory was short: "The $5 SPACE INVADERS™ Cash Invasion Scratch-off launched with a technical issue. We halted the sales of the ticket to ensure the game experience upholds the integrity we strive to provide." What it never publicly detailed is exactly how the misprint happened — but the effect was unambiguous. Players scratched tickets that visually showed prize-winning combinations, then scanned or cashed them and learned the central validation system disagreed.

That gap — between what a ticket looks like and what the lottery's computer says it is — is the heart of every lottery technical glitch dispute. Under Indiana lottery rules, as in most states, the printed symbols are a security layer; the final authority is the game's central validation system. When the two disagree because of a manufacturer's printing error, the apparent "win" doesn't legally exist. What happens next is a question of goodwill, public pressure, and how much of a mess the lottery wants on the front page.

The $100,000 jackpot that wasn't — Mike Fields' story

Mike Fields is the face of the Hoosier Lottery technical issue. His Space Invaders ticket appeared to hit the game's $100,000 grand prize. He did what the lottery tells every big winner to do: he took the ticket straight to headquarters in downtown Indianapolis. Instead of a six-figure check, he was told the ticket had won $20 and that the game was experiencing a system glitch.

When FOX 59 anchor Angela Ganote shared his story on Facebook, the comment section delivered a unanimous verdict — "It says get a rocket, win prize instantly. Sounds like they owe the man $100K!" The public sympathy mattered. Within weeks the lottery had pulled the game, opened protest forms, and by August it was paying four-figure settlements to the affected players instead of the $20 the machines originally offered.

The compromise nobody loved but nobody litigated: tickets that appeared to show $100,000 were settled at $10,000. The lottery framed it as exceeding its legal obligations; players framed it as partial justice for a mistake the lottery's own vendor made.

If you bought the $5 Space Invaders Cash Invasion scratch-off in June 2026 and believe yours was affected by the technical issue, the window is closing:

  • Don't throw the ticket away. A misprinted ticket is your only evidence; the goodwill schedule pays based on what the ticket appears to show.
  • File the protest form before November 30, 2026. The official Space Invaders protest form is available as a PDF on hoosierlottery.com.
  • Call first if you're unsure: Hoosier Lottery player line, 1-800-955-6886.

What the Hoosier Lottery glitch teaches every scratch-off player

The Space Invaders Cash Invasion episode is rare — most technical issues in lottery land are quieter: a retailer terminal freezing mid-cash-in, a scanner misreading a barcode, an app outage during a drawing. But the lessons are the same whether the glitch is worth $20 or $100,000:

  • The screen beats the ticket. Every lottery's central validation system outranks the printed play symbols. If a retailer scan and the ticket disagree, the scan wins until a claim center says otherwise.
  • Sign and keep everything. A signed, photographed, un-damaged ticket is leverage in any dispute — protest forms, chargebacks, small-claims court all start with the physical ticket.
  • Errors do get settled. The Hoosier Lottery paid 252 players above its legal obligation after public pressure. Documented, vocal, organized players move these outcomes.
  • Deadlines are brutal. Protest windows (here, Nov. 30, 2026) and prize-claim deadlines never bend for anyone who "meant to get around to it."

Hoosier Lottery technical issue — FAQ

Straight answers to the questions players searched for all summer.

What was the Hoosier Lottery technical issue?
The $5 Space Invaders Cash Invasion scratch-off launched in June 2026 with a technical issue — a printing error that made some play symbols on losing tickets look like winning matches. The Hoosier Lottery halted sales within days of launch to protect game integrity.
Why did the Hoosier Lottery pull the Space Invaders scratch-off?
The lottery stated the ticket "launched with a technical issue" and that sales were halted "to ensure the game experience upholds the integrity we strive to provide." Misprinted symbols had led multiple players to believe they had won prizes — including the $100,000 top prize — that the lottery's validation system could not confirm.
Did the man who thought he won $100,000 get paid?
Mike Fields was originally told his ticket was worth $20, not the $100,000 top prize he believed he had won. In August 2026 the lottery announced goodwill payments: tickets that appeared to show the $100,000 prize received $10,000 — above what Indiana law requires, according to lottery officials.
How much is the Hoosier Lottery paying for misprinted tickets?
For the 252 confirmed misprinted tickets: $10,000 for tickets that appeared to show $100,000; $500 for tickets that appeared to show $2,500–$5,900; $100 for tickets that appeared to show $500–$2,499; and $25 for tickets that appeared to show $10–$499.
How do I file a Hoosier Lottery protest form?
Download the Space Invaders protest form from hoosierlottery.com, complete it, and submit it by the November 30, 2026 deadline. For help, call the Hoosier Lottery at 1-800-955-6886.
Was the payout required by Indiana law?
No. Lottery officials said the goodwill payments exceed what Indiana law requires and are not funded by taxpayer dollars. They are voluntary payments to resolve the misprint dispute.
Can a lottery refuse to pay a jackpot because of a technical issue?
Yes. A ticket is only a winner if the lottery's central validation system confirms it — the printed symbols are a security feature, not the final word. When a game error creates apparent winners, lotteries typically pull the game, investigate, and offer a settlement; that is exactly the process the Hoosier Lottery followed here.
